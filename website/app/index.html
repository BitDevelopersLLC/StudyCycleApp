    How it works

    Pick one task and focus on it until the timer ends. Then take a real break, away from your screen. Repeat.

    Study Cycle tells you when each focus block and break ends. In this web version that works while this tab is open. The iPhone and Android app also alerts you when your phone is locked.

    Why 90 minutes?

    Sleep researcher Nathaniel Kleitman proposed that a roughly 90-minute "basic rest-activity cycle" continues through the day as well as during sleep.

    In a well-known study of expert violinists, the best players practiced in sessions of about an hour to an hour and a half, with breaks in between, and rarely more than four hours a day.

    A 2022 review of 22 study groups (2,335 people) found that short breaks give a small boost in energy and reduce tiredness. Their effect on performance was clear only for less demanding tasks.

    There is no proof that exactly 90 minutes is best for everyone. Use it as a starting point, and try the 50- or 25-minute cycles, or set your own.

    References

    1. Kleitman, N. (1982). Basic rest-activity cycle—22 years later. Sleep, 5(4), 311–317. doi.org/10.1093/sleep/5.4.311
    2. Ericsson, K. A., Krampe, R. T., & Tesch-Römer, C. (1993). The role of deliberate practice in the acquisition of expert performance. Psychological Review, 100(3), 363–406. doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.100.3.363
    3. Albulescu, P., et al. (2022). "Give me a break!" A systematic review and meta-analysis on the efficacy of micro-breaks for increasing well-being and performance. PLoS ONE, 17(8), e0272460. doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0272460

    Your privacy

    Study Cycle collects no data. There are no ads, accounts, cookies, analytics or trackers, and this page loads nothing from other websites.

    Your settings and progress are stored only in this browser, on this device. Clearing this site's data in your browser deletes them, and so does "Delete my progress".

What it does

A simple timer that helps you study in focused blocks and actually rest between them.

Focus and break timer

Deep focus (90 minutes, then a 20-minute break) is the default. Or pick Classic (50 / 10), Short (25 / 5) or Custom: 5 to 180 minutes of focus and 1 to 60 minutes of break.

Alerts when time's up

Study Cycle tells you when each focus block or break ends, even when your phone is locked.

Keeps the right time

The timer stays correct if you close the app or your phone restarts during a session.

Break ideas

Each break suggests something away from the screen: stretch, drink some water, rest your eyes, or take a short walk.

Your progress

Today's focus time, a 7-day chart, your streak and all-time totals. Stored only on your phone, and you can delete it anytime.

Works your way

Pause, resume or skip to the break anytime. Optionally start the next focus block automatically (it waits for you after 3 in a row) or keep the screen on.

Easy to read

Light and dark mode, screen reader labels, and text that grows with your phone's font size.

Free and open source

No price, no subscription, no ads. The code is public, so anyone can check what it does.

How it works

Three steps, repeated as many times as you like.

  1. Pick a cycle

    Start with Deep focus (90 minutes of focus, then 20 minutes of rest), or choose Classic, Short or your own lengths.

  2. Focus on one task

    Tap Start focus and stay with one thing. Notes and questions can wait for the break. Study Cycle tells you when the time is up.

  3. Take a real break

    Step away from your screen and try the break idea. When the break ends, start the next cycle, or let it start by itself.

Why 90 minutes?

A starting point with research behind it, not a rule.

Sleep researcher Nathaniel Kleitman proposed that a roughly 90-minute "basic rest-activity cycle" continues through the day as well as during sleep.

In a well-known study of expert violinists, the best players practiced in sessions of about an hour to an hour and a half, with breaks in between, and rarely more than four hours a day.

A 2022 review of 22 study groups (2,335 people) found that short breaks give a small boost in energy and reduce tiredness. Their effect on performance was clear only for less demanding tasks.

There is no proof that exactly 90 minutes is best for everyone. Use it as a starting point, and try the 50- or 25-minute cycles, or set your own.

Alerts

My alerts arrive late on Android. How do I fix that?

On Android 14 and later, you choose whether Study Cycle may use Alarms & reminders. Without it, alerts can arrive late while your phone is idle.

  • If the app shows "Alerts may arrive late", tap Allow and turn on Alarms & reminders for Study Cycle.
  • You can also search your phone's Settings for Alarms & reminders.

Some phones also pause apps to save battery. If alerts are still late, go to Settings > Apps > Study Cycle > Battery and choose Unrestricted (the name varies by phone).

I don't get any alerts. Are notifications turned off?

Study Cycle needs permission to show notifications, and your phone asks you first. If notifications are off, the app tells you on its main screen. To turn them on:

  • iPhone: open Settings > Notifications > Study Cycle and turn on Allow Notifications.
  • Android: open Settings > Apps > Study Cycle > Notifications and turn them on.

Also check that your phone isn't muted.

Will I get alerts with Do Not Disturb or a Focus turned on?

Do Not Disturb on Android and Focus modes on iPhone can silence or hide notifications, including Study Cycle's. To get its alerts while you study, allow Study Cycle as an exception.

  • iPhone: go to Settings > Focus, pick the Focus you use, tap Apps, and add Study Cycle.
  • Android: search your phone's Settings for Do Not Disturb and add Study Cycle to the apps that can interrupt (the steps vary by phone).
Does the timer keep going if I close the app or my phone restarts?

Yes. Study Cycle saves the time when the current focus block or break ends, so it shows the right time whenever you open it again, even after a restart. Its alerts are scheduled on your phone ahead of time, so they arrive while your phone is locked.

Your data

Where is my progress stored? Can I back it up or delete it?

Your settings and study totals are stored only on your phone. There's no account, so nothing syncs between devices.

  • To delete your study totals, open Progress and tap Delete my progress.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes everything.
  • On iPhone, your data may be included in your own device backup if you have turned that on. Android app backup is turned off for Study Cycle.
Does the app collect any data?

No. There's no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. The Android app doesn't even have permission to use the internet. The privacy policy lists every permission and what it's for.
